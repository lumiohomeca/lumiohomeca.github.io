Level 01 · Beginner

Build the foundation.

Three essential lessons before choosing your first serious watch.

BEGINNER · LESSON 01

How a watch keeps time.

A movement is the engine. It stores or receives energy, divides time into measured intervals, and moves the hands. The big question is where that energy comes from.

BEGINNER · LESSON 02

Mechanical, automatic or quartz?

A manual mechanical watch is wound by hand. An automatic winds through wrist motion. Quartz uses a battery and a vibrating crystal for accuracy. None is universally “best”—the right one depends on use and feeling.

BEGINNER · LESSON 03

What specifications matter.

Case diameter affects fit; sapphire resists scratches; water resistance describes real limits; and a seller’s warranty and returns matter as much as the reference itself.

BEGINNER · LESSON 04

Water resistance, without the confusion.

“Water resistant” is not a single promise. Read the exact rating, the crown design and the manufacturer’s instructions. Heat, steam and an open crown can compromise a watch even when its rating sounds reassuring.

BEGINNER · LESSON 05

Finding the right fit.

Diameter is only the start. Consider lug-to-lug length, thickness, bracelet taper and your wrist shape. A well-proportioned 38 mm watch can feel more substantial than a poorly fitted 42 mm watch.

BEGINNER · LESSON 06

Care from day one.

Keep the crown secure, wipe the case and bracelet after wear, and do not treat magnetic fields or impacts casually. Good care protects both daily enjoyment and eventual resale condition.

Level 02 · Intermediate

Develop your point of view.

Learn to read a watch’s design language and position with more confidence.

INTERMEDIATE · LESSON 01

Dress, field, diver or chronograph?

Dress watches are restrained; field watches favour legibility; divers prioritise water resistance; chronographs add timing functions. Categories help explain a watch’s intended character.

INTERMEDIATE · LESSON 02

What a brand can signal.

Brands differ in design language, heritage, movement choices, service network and price positioning. A logo is not a specification; look at the exact reference and what it gives you.

INTERMEDIATE · LESSON 03

Complications, explained simply.

A complication is any function beyond displaying hours, minutes and seconds: date, GMT, chronograph, calendar or moonphase. More functions can add usefulness, but also change thickness, cost and servicing needs.

INTERMEDIATE · LESSON 04

Steel, titanium, gold and ceramic.

Material changes more than appearance. Steel is versatile, titanium is light, gold has warmth and presence, and ceramic offers exceptional scratch resistance. The best choice is the one that suits how you actually wear it.

INTERMEDIATE · LESSON 05

Straps change the whole watch.

A bracelet makes a watch more architectural; leather can make it quieter and more formal; rubber shifts it toward active use. Learn the lug width and you can make one good watch feel like several.

Level 03 · Collector

Choose with context.

A collector’s habit is comparing the complete reference, not simply the name on the dial.

COLLECTOR · LESSON 01

Choose with context.

Start with budget, wrist size and the occasions you dress for. Then compare exact references, current price and seller terms—not just a brand name.

COLLECTOR · LESSON 02

Service is part of ownership.

Mechanical watches need periodic professional attention, while quartz watches need battery and seal care. Before buying, look for service access, expected costs and the strength of the warranty in Canada.

COLLECTOR · LESSON 03

Condition, completeness and provenance.

Original parts, a clear seller history, box and papers, and honest condition notes all help you evaluate a watch. Documentation cannot replace judgment, but it makes comparison much more disciplined.

COLLECTOR · LESSON 04

Value is not the same as resale.

A watch can be excellent value because it fits your life, wears beautifully and is easy to service. Future resale is uncertain—treat it as a secondary consideration, never as a guarantee.
